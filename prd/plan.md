    ---
    Industry UX Research: Best Practices

    Critical Pages/Features:
    1. Homepage with prominent YMM search front and center
    2. Category/Collection pages with faceted filtering
    3. Product detail pages with fitment verification, multiple images, specs, install info, reviews
    4. Vehicle-specific landing pages (e.g., "2022 Ford F-150 Tonneau Covers") — critical for SEO
    5. My Garage / Saved Vehicles for returning customers
    6. Installation guides / resources section

    Conversion-Driving Patterns:
    - Site speed = ~50% of predicted conversion rate (Hedges & Company). 3-second load = 1.8% conversion vs 0.5% at 8 seconds
    - Fitment confirmation badges — green checkmark "This fits your [vehicle]"
    - Simplified checkout with guest checkout and Apple Pay/Google Pay/Shop Pay
    - Multiple product angles + photos of parts installed on vehicles
    - Spec comparison tables between similar products (most sites fail at this per Baymard)
    - Dynamic recommendations based on selected vehicle

    Year/Make/Model Fitment Implementation:
    - Sequential dropdown: Year → Make → Model → Submodel/Trim
    - Persistent filter across entire site once selected
    - "FITS" / "DOES NOT FIT" indicators on product pages
    - Alternative entry: VIN lookup
    - Shopify apps: Convermax ($250-850/mo), PCFitment, Instant Fit
    - Data standards: ACES (fitment) and PIES (product attributes)

    Trust Signals That Matter Most:
    1. Fitment guarantee — "#1 anxiety reducer"
    2. Customer reviews with install photos (92% trust non-paid recommendations)
    3. Security badges on checkout
    4. Transparent pricing — no surprise fees
    5. Physical business address and phone number
    6. Clear return/warranty policy accessible from every product page
    7. Brand logos — "Authorized dealer of..."

    Common Mistakes to Avoid:
    1. Poor/missing fitment data — #1 source of returns and chargebacks
    2. Low-quality or single-angle product images
    3. Rotating homepage banners/sliders — shoppers ignore them, they slow load times
    4. Complex checkout requiring account creation
    5. Ignoring mobile (70-80% of traffic is mobile)
    6. Inadequate product descriptions (no specs, dimensions, materials)
    7. No comparison support (most common UX gap per Baymard)
    8. Slow site speed
    9. No vehicle-specific SEO landing pages
    10. No "My Garage" / saved vehicle feature

    Recommended Category Structure:
    - Tonneau Covers — Folding (hard/soft), Roll-up, Retractable, One-piece
    - Bull Bars & Bumper Guards — Bull bars, Sport bars
    - Grille Guards & Brush Guards — Full grille guards, Brush guards, Winch mount
    - Side Steps & Running Boards — Nerf bars, Running boards, Power/electric, Step bars
    - Trailer Hitches & Towing — Receiver hitches (by class), Accessories, Wiring harnesses
    - Secondary nav: Shop by Vehicle (Make → Model → Year)
    - Tertiary nav: Shop by Brand (alphabetical with logos)

    ---
    Key Takeaways for Stehlen Auto

    1. Fitment-first design — YMM search is the most prominent homepage element
    2. Speed over features — target under 3 seconds load time
    3. Mobile-first — design for phones first, scale up to desktop
    4. Vehicle-specific landing pages for SEO capture
    5. Comparison tools = competitive advantage (most sites do this poorly)
    6. Lifestyle-first brand copy — sell the adventure, not just the part
    7. Customer install photos — build community and trust simultaneously

    Industry conversion benchmark: ~2.0% average, top performers reach up to 16%.
